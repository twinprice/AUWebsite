Five logo directions for Acoustic Underground

Each concept is shown at three scales: large on dark (hero/poster), on light (print, merch), and small on dark (favicon, social avatar). The goal with each is to feel nothing like a credit card.

01

Horizon — serif wordmark with a sound-wave line

Fraunces italic wordmark, a literal sound wave acting as the horizon line dividing name from city. Literary, cinematic, no circle, no sphere. The wave references "acoustic" without being corny. Reads as a folk-rock band that takes its songwriting seriously.

02

Letterpress block — bold gig-poster energy

Borrows from woodblock concert posters. Heavy sans on top, serif italic below, hard box frame. Photographs well on merch, looks great stamped in a single color, and immediately reads as a band, not a brand. The frame is the container, not a circle.

03

Square monogram — architectural and confident

A square (not a circle) holds an "AU" ligature formed from negative space. The band name sits alongside in all-caps condensed letters with extreme tracking. Modern indie rock feel. The square breaks the credit-card association completely. Scales down to 16px favicon.

04

Underground horizon — heavy serif with a literal horizon

"acoustic" sits above a horizon line made of the Richmond skyline silhouette. "Underground" sits below, a true underground. Plays on the band name without being cutesy. No circle anywhere. Works beautifully at poster size; the skyline simplifies to a single line at small scales.

05

Concert poster — stacked typographic lockup

Full 70s concert-bill treatment. "EST" date rule, oversized italic display, ornamental "and" ligature, heavy slab below, city in small caps at the base. Pure typography, zero shapes. Photographs like a Rolling Stone tour poster. Strong at large sizes; at small sizes you drop all but the two main words.
