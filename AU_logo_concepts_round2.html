Round 2: five more directions, all contained

You liked the grounded feel of a container but not a circle. These five all live inside a defined shape, and all draw from the band's actual identity: Richmond bar-and-brewery band since 2020, harmonies, covers + originals, "play it well, make it fun."

06

Ticket stub — a band you can hold in your hand

A rounded rectangle with two notches that reads as a torn concert ticket. Reinforces live music at a glance. Typography is Playfair italic (goosebumps territory) with Oswald condensed for the supporting type. Works on a stage banner, a koozie, or a 32px favicon.

Pointed crest — heritage without being historical

A five-sided shield shape (flat top, pointed bottom). Same "established" weight as a crest or brewery logo, but the narrow point keeps it modern and forward-leaning rather than old-world. Abril Fatface gives it literary gravity. This is the logo of a band that's been doing it a while.

08

Offset frame — print-shop precision with a little wink

Two rectangles offset by a few pixels, the way a letterpress print looks when the color plate registers slightly off. Signals craft and attention to detail (your actual positioning). Feels hand-printed without being twee. The offset is the personality.

09

Arch — a stage you walk under

An arc opens across the top while the bottom stays flat. Echoes a stage proscenium, a doorway into a club, or a horizon from above. Contained but not closed. Playfair italic for "Acoustic," bold Playfair for "Underground." The amber rule underneath anchors the whole mark to the ground.

Cassette nameplate — music object, not band logo
