Round 5: the A and U hidden in the waves

Sound waveforms naturally curve in ways that can form letterforms. Here are three variations that hide a clear A and U inside the wave pattern itself, so the icon reads as both "harmonies/sound waves" AND as the band's initials, depending on how you look at it. This is the same trick the Rolling Stones tongue pulls (a mouth AND an attitude) or the FedEx arrow (text AND a forward motion).

22

Two-wave AU — the cleanest read

Two sound waves stacked. The top wave is shaped to trace an A (peaks up into a triangular crest, with a horizontal crossbar). The bottom wave is shaped to trace a U (dips down into a rounded valley, with the ends curving up). Together they read as waves, as AU, and as harmony. The crossbar of the A is the key move, it makes the A unmistakable instead of just "a peak."

One continuous waveform — the A flows into the U

A single unbroken line traces an A on the left, then continues seamlessly into a U on the right. Reads as one waveform that happens to spell your initials. The three approaches in a sentence each:

· 22 — Two-wave AU: Cleanest read. The A and U are each their own waveform. Strongest separation, easiest to recognize.
· 23 — Strings bending: Most literal to "acoustic" since the strings are the hook. The AU emerges from the strings bending together.
· 24 — One continuous line: Most elegant. A flows directly into U without a break. Subtlest; the AU is a reward for looking twice.

My honest take: 22 is the strongest as a band icon. It does the AU trick AND still reads clearly as "two waves" (harmonies) at a glance. 23 is cool but the background strings compete with the letters. 24 is beautiful but the AU might be too hidden to deliver the punch you want.
